### [Food Labeling: Nutrient Content Claims; Definition of Term “Healthy”](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/02/25/2025-03118/food-labeling-nutrient-content-claims-definition-of-term-healthy)

`HIGH` · Final rule · Health and Human Services Department, Food and Drug Administration · 25 Feb 2025 · 90 FR 10592

- This is a delay of the effective date only: FDA's final rule "Food Labeling: Nutrient Content Claims; Definition of Term 'Healthy'" (89 FR 106064, published December 27, 2024) now takes effect April 28, 2025 instead of February 25, 2025.
- The compliance date of the healthy rule is unchanged and is stated in the text as 2028; the document says parties may begin implementing the rule earlier.
- The healthy rule updates when 'healthy' can be used as an implied nutrient content claim, uses food group equivalents (vegetables, fruits, dairy, grains, protein foods, oils from the Dietary Guidelines 2020-2025) and limits on added sugars, saturated fat and sodium as a percentage of Daily Value, and requires records kept at least 2 years where the FGE is not apparent from the label.
- The delay follows the January 20, 2025 presidential 'Regulatory Freeze Pending Review' memorandum, and FDA issued it without notice and comment under good-cause provisions.

FDA is delaying the effective date of its December 27, 2024 final rule defining the nutrient content claim 'healthy' (21 CFR 101), from February 25, 2025 to April 28, 2025. The delay implements the January 20, 2025 presidential memorandum directing agencies to consider postponing effective dates of rules that were published but not yet in effect, so officials can review questions of fact, law and policy.

For a company designing its own labels, the practical change is a two-month shift in when the new 'healthy' claim requirements become effective, while the compliance date stays unchanged at 2028 according to the document. Labels using 'healthy' should still be planned against the rule's requirements for FGEs, added sugar, saturated fat and sodium limits, automatic-qualifying foods and recordkeeping. The document does not state whether the rule will be further changed after the review.

### [21 CFR § 101.65](https://www.ecfr.gov/on/2025-04-28/title-21/section-101.65) · Amended

§ 101.65 Implied nutrient content claims and related label statements. · in effect from 28 Apr 2025 · +74/−26 lines · [side-by-side](https://www.ecfr.gov/compare/2025-04-28/to/2025-04-27/title-21/section-101.65)

**The 2025 amendment rewrites the "healthy" implied claim in 21 CFR 101.65(d): it replaces the old per-food fat, saturated fat, cholesterol and sodium tables with a food group equivalent framework, new Tables 2 through 6 with added sugars, sodium and saturated fat limits in %DV, and a mandatory written-records requirement (kept at least 2 years). It also exempts paragraph (d) claims from the § 101.13(h) cholesterol disclosure requirement in paragraph (a)(2).** (relevance 92/100)

For a packaged-food company using "healthy" on labels, this is the most consequential change: products must now be checked against food group equivalents and the category tables (added sugars, sodium and saturated fat as %DV per RACC or per labeled serving for mixed, main dish and meal products) rather than the old fat and sodium thresholds, and the company must maintain formulation or batch records proving food group equivalent compliance, available to FDA on request. Labels that relied on the former criteria will need re-evaluation, and the cholesterol disclosure tie-in under 101.13(h) no longer applies to paragraph (d) claims. The text provided does not state a compliance date for the new criteria, so that should be confirmed from the Federal Register notice.

### [21 CFR § 101.13](https://www.ecfr.gov/on/2025-04-28/title-21/section-101.13) · Amended

§ 101.13 Nutrient content claims—general principles. · in effect from 28 Apr 2025 · +1/−1 lines · [side-by-side](https://www.ecfr.gov/compare/2025-04-28/to/2025-04-27/title-21/section-101.13)

**The implied "healthy-type" claim definition in § 101.13(b)(2)(ii) was reworded: the text now says a food may be described as useful in maintaining healthy dietary practices where there is implied or explicit nutrition content information, replacing the requirement that such a claim be made in association with an explicit nutrient statement (e.g., "healthy, contains 3 grams of fat"). The example now reads only "healthy".** (relevance 72/100)

For a small packaged-food company, this loosens the test for when a general "healthy" word counts as an implied nutrient content claim: the old wording required a paired explicit nutrient statement, while the new wording turns on whether there is implied or explicit nutrition information about the food. Labels using "healthy" should be rechecked against the new trigger, and the company should confirm that the nutrition panel and any healthy-related claims still meet the requirements for nutrient content claims, including the disclosure and criteria rules in paragraphs (h), (i) and (p). The text itself does not state any compliance deadline or other conditions beyond the effective date given.
